# Stakeholder session findings

## Purpose and method

The stakeholder engagement session gathered early feedback on informed-consent
workflows for field research. This summary is based on the anonymized team
feedback summary and its feedback-board synthesis; it does not reproduce raw
notes, recordings, names, or identifying details.

The available summary reports feedback from nine respondents, most of whom
were researchers. It does not provide enough detail to verify the recruitment
process, exact session format, or the questions asked. Confirm those details
against the approved session record before using this report as a formal
methods account. Do not add identifying source material to the repository.

## Findings reported by participants

The small respondent group raised these recurring concerns:

- **Length and difficulty:** 7 of 9 respondents described consent forms as
  long or difficult to use.
- **Consent involving children:** 5 of 9 raised guardian/child consent issues
  and related ethics delays.
- **Language and literacy:** 3 of 9 raised language barriers, and 3 of 9
  raised literacy concerns.
- **Field conditions:** 3 of 9 raised connectivity or power constraints.

Other feedback themes included the need for plain-language explanations of
consent and participant rights; better discoverability of language and
read-aloud support; simpler visual presentation; clear approval and
translation sign-off states; reliable offline recovery; and audio records
linked to the correct consent record. Paper-form import and guidance for
witnessed consent were raised as possibilities to explore.

These counts are descriptive counts from the available summary. Themes may
overlap; the counts should not be added together or interpreted as
representative estimates.

## Design implications

The team can use these findings to guide design exploration:

1. Prioritize concise, plain-language consent content and make participant
   rights easy to locate and understand.
2. Make language selection and read-aloud support visible, while preserving
   human review and sign-off for translated content.
3. Reduce visual and interaction complexity, then validate changes with
   participants who have varied literacy and device experience.
4. Make translation and ethics approval status explicit in the researcher
   workflow. Any proposed turnaround target, including a three-day target,
   remains an aspiration until the responsible stakeholders validate it.
5. Test offline operation, interrupted-session recovery, and reliable links
   between audio records and the corresponding consent record.
6. Treat paper-form import and witnessed-consent guidance as questions for
   further workflow and ethics validation, not established requirements.

These are design hypotheses informed by stakeholder feedback; they are not
evidence that a specific design change will solve the reported problems.

## Limitations

This was a small, researcher-heavy group. No ethics-office staff or
interpreters were represented in the feedback summarized here. The findings
are early qualitative signals, not a representative survey, a fairness
assessment, or proof that the proposed design implications are effective.
The summary does not establish the prevalence of these issues across
institutions, regions, languages, or participant populations.

Follow-up should include usability sessions with participants who have varied
language, literacy, and device experience, and consultation with ethics and
translation stakeholders. Report those results separately from this
stakeholder summary, with their own methods, sample limitations, and privacy
review.

## Privacy and use

This report intentionally contains no respondent-level data or direct
identifiers. Keep raw notes, recordings, contact details, and any key that
could reconnect feedback to a person in the team's approved restricted
storage—not in Git, issues, or public reports.
